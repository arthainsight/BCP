# The Phaladīpikā beside the app: Ṣaḍbala, the daśās, and what it confirms

Read on 31 October 2026 in `phaldipika_202310` (the Sanskrit verses with the Hindi commentary of Hari Shankar Pathak, Chaukhambha) and, for the Jātakapārijāta, in
`jataka-parijata` (Hindi). Chapter 4 (the six strengths) and chapters 19 to 22 (the daśās) were read; the rest of the book is not. The OCR is good but not perfect; where a
figure rests on a reading of the OCR it says so. The decodings of the Kālachakra sentences are the session's own (a kaṭapayādi decoder), the lists of signs under them are
those of the editor.

## The Ṣaḍbala of the Phaladīpikā (chapter 4)

Mantreśvara's six strengths are **not** the six of BPHS 27: Kāla, Ceṣṭā, Ucca, Dig, Ayana and Sthāna (4.1–3), against the BPHS's Sthāna, Dig, Kāla, Ceṣṭā, Naisargika and Dṛk. The Ucca
and the Ayana stand on their own; the Naisargika is a part of the Sthāna; the Dṛk is not among the six. The strengths are told in words, with no virūpa tables (only the
fractions below), so the book gives no numbers to compare the app's tables with.

| Part | Phaladīpikā | BPHS 27 / the app |
|---|---|---|
| Dig (4.2) | Sun and Mars in the 10th, Moon and Venus in the 4th, Mercury and Jupiter in the Lagna, Saturn in the 7th | the same |
| Kāla, day and night (4.1) | Mars, Moon and **Venus** by night, Mercury always, Sun, Jupiter and **Saturn** by day | the Natonnata of BPHS has Moon, Mars and **Saturn** by night, Sun, Jupiter and **Venus** by day. The Sārāvalī verse the commentary quotes beside it has the BPHS way ("jīvārkāsphujito'hni vit ca satatam mandendubhaumā niśi"); Mantreśvara's verse differs from both |
| Pakṣa (4.1) | the benefics in the bright half, the malefics in the dark half, Mercury with the company it keeps | the same idea (the Moon's Pakṣa Bala and the Pakṣa Bala of all) |
| Year, month, day, hora (4.1) | each graha in its own, "aṅghrivṛddhyā": by quarters | the same: 15, 30, 45, 60 virūpa |
| Ceṣṭā (4.2) | the full Moon, the Sun in the northern course, a retrograde graha, the winner in a planetary war, a graha with its full rays | the idea of BPHS 27, with no table |
| Ucca (4.2) | in its own exaltation | a part of the Sthāna in BPHS and the app |
| Ayana (4.2) | Mercury, Saturn and the Moon in the southern course; the others in the northern (the Hindi gloss has Mercury in both lists) | the table or the declination of the app (`shadbala-sources.md`) |
| Sthāna (4.3) | exaltation, own sign, a friend's sign, the six vargas; the angles full, the succedent houses a half, the cadent a quarter; males strong at the start of a sign, neuters in the middle, females at the end | the Sapta-vargaja, Kendrādi and Drekkāṇa Bala of BPHS: the same 1 : ½ : ¼ (60, 30, 15) and the same drekkāṇa idea |
| Naisargika (4.3) | Saturn, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Moon, Sun in rising order | the same (8.57 to 60) |
| Lagna Bala (4.6) | a male (odd) sign strong, Scorpio a quarter, the others a half; the lord in an upacaya house; a night-strong sign by night, a day-strong sign by day | not in the app |
| Bala by sign (4.7) | exalted full, mūlatrikoṇa ¾, own sign ½, friend ¼, enemy little, debilitated or combust weak | a different scale from the Sapta-vargaja of BPHS (30, 22.5, 15…) |
| The angles (4.8) | rising in the order 4th (¼), 10th (½), 7th (¾), Lagna (full); the Laghu Pārāśarī, quoted beside it, has them rising 1, 4, 7, 10 | all angles alike in BPHS |
| A graha that is retrograde or has its full rays (4.4) | strong even in its fall or an enemy's navāṁśa; a combust graha weak even in its exaltation | the Ceṣṭā of the retrograde and the combustion of the app work apart from this |
| **The least total (4.22–23)** | Sun 6½, Moon 6, Mars 5, Mercury 7, Jupiter 6½, Venus 5½, Saturn 5 rūpa | **the same as BPHS 27** |
| Bhāva Bala (4.24) | the lord's total, the Dig Bala and the Dṛg Bala of the house, and 1 rūpa | BPHS 27 has the same parts |

Not in the app, in the same chapter (4.12–20): the **Candra-kriyā, Candra-avasthā and Candra-velā**: the part of the birth nakṣatra that has passed is divided into 60, 12 and 36
shares (of 13°20′ over 60, 12 and 36), each with a result of its own (three tables of 60, 12 and 36 results), for the birth chart, the muhūrta and the question.

## The daśās (chapters 13, 19, 20 and 22)

**Confirmed**
* **Viṃśottarī (19.2):** the years 6, 10, 7, 18, 16, 19, 17, 7 and 20 from the Sun in the order Sun, Moon, Mars, Rāhu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, Ketu, Venus, from Kṛttikā. **Mercury is 17**,
  as in the app; the 19 that BPHS 46.15 prints for Mercury is the slip that `dasha-vs-sources.md` takes it for.
* **Kālachakra (22.1–9):** the 27 nakṣatras in nine groups of three; in Mantreśvara's names the five odd groups (15 nakṣatras: Aśvinī to Kṛttikā, Punarvasu to Āśleṣā, Hasta to Svāti, Mūla to
  Uttara Āṣāḍhā, Pūrva Bhādrapada to Revatī) are the *apasavya* and the four even groups (12 nakṣatras) the *savya* (the names are the other way round from BPHS 46: the editor says
  so). The two sets of stars are the same as the four groups of BPHS in the app put together in pairs (10 + 5 = 15 and 4 + 8 = 12 stars), and **Revatī stands with Kṛttikā, Āśleṣā, Svāti and
  Uttara Āṣāḍhā**, as the fix of v3.65 has it, from a text that is not BPHS.
* **The sixteen sequences of nine signs (22, the vākyas):** the first eight sentences were decoded with the kaṭapayādi rule (ka, ṭa, pa, ya = 1; ṅa, ṇa, ma, śa = 5; na and ña = 0, and in these
  sentences na = 10, kṣa = 11 and tra = 12) and seven of the eight give, as printed, the sequences of the first savya and second savya groups of the app (`kalachakra.test.ts`); the third ("rūpotarakṣurnidhāyaraṅgam") is spoilt by an OCR slip ("tara" for "tra") and gives the app's sequence 2, 1, 12, 11, 10, 9, 1, 2, 3 once that is mended; the ninth, "gośivatīrthe
  dātrīkṣanno", gives 3, 5, 4, 6, 7, 8, 12, 11, 10, the third pada of the second apasavya group of the app. The editor's own decoded lists of all sixteen sequences agree with the app in every readable part in 15 cases (three lists lose a sign or two to the OCR) and
  in the sixteenth have the end 12, 1, 2 where the sentence and the app have 12, 11, 10: a slip of the editor's table (12, 1, 2 is the end of the line above it).
* **Naisargika and Piṇḍāyu (Jātakapārijāta, chapter 5):** the natural years Sun 20, Moon 1, Mars 2, Mercury 9, Jupiter 18, Venus 20, Saturn 50 and the Piṇḍāyu years 19, 25, 15, 12, 15, 21, 20, as in
  BPHS 43 (the commentary quotes the Sārāvalī for them). The commentary also gives the rule of BPHS 43.8 in words and with a worked example: subtract the exaltation point from the graha;
  if the rest is under six signs, take it from twelve signs; in minutes, times the years, over 21 600. That is the line from the half at the fall to the whole at the exaltation, the reading
  of `ayurdaya.ts`.

**In the app since v3.72:** the Kālachakra condition (22.30) and the choice of the span of life (22.28–29), as below.

**Which span of life to take (22.28–29):** with a strong Lagna the **Aṃśāyu**, with a strong Sun the **Piṇḍāyu**, with a strong Moon the **Naisargika**; when the three are equal in strength a third of their
sum, when two are equal half of the sum of those two, and when none is strong the span of Jīvaśarman. This settles the "choice between the three spans (BPHS 43.31–32)" that
`ayurdaya-vs-sources.md` lists as open. **What the app does with it (v3.72, `lifespanMethod.ts`):** the panels of the Paiṇḍī, the Aṃśī and the Naisargika daśā say which span the text takes for the chart, by the strongest
of the Lagna, the Sun and the Moon (the strength of the signs they are in, by the Jaimini rules; two are equal when they are in the same sign, and then the mean of their spans is named).
The app has no summed span of life (the classical total of the grahas' years is not worked out), so the mean is named and not computed; "none is strong: Jīvaśarman" is not applied.

**A condition for the Kālachakra (22.30):** "the Kālachakra is to be taken when the lord of the Moon's navāṁśa is strong; the daśā that rests on the nakṣatras (Viṃśottarī) is always strong". **Done in v3.72
(`kalachakraCondition.ts`):** the Kālachakra panel states the condition and whether the chart meets it, and shows the daśā only when it does (or on request, "Calculate anyway"); the event
list, the finder and the overlap read it only when it does. The PyJHora way, always, is a choice (Settings → Kalachakra). The text does not say what "strong" is; the Phaladīpikā's own account of
strength (4.3–4) is followed: strong in its exaltation, own or mūlatrikoṇa sign or a friend's sign, a retrograde graha strong whatever its sign, a combust one weak even in its exaltation, a debilitated
one weak. (The editor reads the verse so; the Sanskrit "candrāṁśeśe balānvite" is short.)

**Named, not given:** chapter 13.25 names the daśās for the length of life: that "told by Śrīpati", the **Aṣṭakavarga daśā**, the Kālachakra and the Uḍu daśā (Viṃśottarī). The editor's note
under 22.1 says that many daśās by graha, sign and nakṣatra are in the jātaka books and that the Phaladīpikā has only a few (his Daśāphaladarpaṇa has the rest). The Jātakapārijāta (chapter 5) names eight kinds of
span: Nisargāyu, Piṇḍāyu, Lagnāyu, Aṃśakāyu, Raśmijāyu, Cakrāyu, Nakṣatrāyu and Aṣṭavargajāyu, of which the app has the first, the second and (as Aṃśī) the fourth; the others are not worked out here.

## The dating of Mantreśvara

The editor says some verses of the Phaladīpikā are the verses of Vaidyanātha's Jātakapārijāta word for word, so that Mantreśvara cannot be of the thirteenth century (Vaidyanātha being later) and
the sixteenth is likelier. This is the editor's claim and has not been tested here.

## Vedha of the transits (26.3–8) — in the app since v3.73

Read in the Sanskrit and the Hindi commentary of the edition (the verses with the Nārada verses quoted under them). Each graha is good in some houses from the natal Moon (26.2, already in the
app) **provided no other graha stands in the vedha house that goes with the good one**. The pairs (good house → vedha house) are in `VEDHA_FROM_MOON` of `src/lib/gochara.ts`; the verses give the two
lists in the same order, and the Hindi commentary writes them out in pairs, which agree with the Nārada verses under each (Sun 11–5, 3–9, 10–4, 6–12; Moon 7–2, 1–5, 6–12, 11–8, 10–4, 3–9; Mars/Saturn
3–12, 11–5, 6–9; Mercury 2–5, 4–3, 6–9, 8–1, 10–8, 11–12; Jupiter 2–12, 11–8, 9–10, 5–4, 7–3; Venus 1–8, 2–7, 3–1, 4–10, 5–9, 8–5, 9–11, 11–3, 12–6).

* **Exceptions in the verses:** the Sun and Saturn do not obstruct each other (26.3, 26.5); nor do the Moon and Mercury (26.4, 26.6). Applied.
* **Rāhu and Ketu** as obstructors: the verses say "any graha" but name neither; left out (`VEDHA_OBSTRUCTORS`). Open.
* **Not read:** how much a blocked good house is worth (the text only says the good result is not given), and whether the vedha applies to the bad houses (it does not: the text gives no vedha for them).
* The edition's OCR garbles the last pair of Jupiter's list ("33"/"aut"); 7↔3 is taken from the other pairs (the 5 good houses, 5 vedha houses) and agrees with the usual table.
