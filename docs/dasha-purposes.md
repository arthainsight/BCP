# What the classics say each daśā is for, and why the Viṃśottarī is the one in use

Searched on 31 October 2026 in the corpus on the session's disk (BPHS in Sanskrit with IAST, the Jaimini Sūtras with the Hindi of Sītārāma Jhā, the Phaladīpikā with the Hindi of
Hari Shankar Pathak, the Jātakapārijāta, the Uttara Kālāmṛta, the Laghu Pārāśarī, the Mānasāgarī and the others of `vedic-classics.md`). Only what a verse or an editor says is written
here; what is from the memory of the writer is marked so. Most daśās are given with no purpose at all, only a rule.

## What a text says a daśā is for

| Daśā | Purpose in the text | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Viṃśottarī | "the principal" (*mukhyā*); the reason given: a full life is 120 years, and in the Kali age the Viṃśottarī, which adds up to 120, is therefore the principal daśā | BPHS 46.3, 46.14 |
| Kālachakra | "honoured among all the daśās" (*mānyā sarvadaśāsu*); the jumps of its wheel (*maṇḍūka*, *markaṭī*, *siṃhāvalokana*) are read for fever, danger to the parents, the death of the father or mother, loss of wealth, falls, fear of the king, and one's own death; the Deha and Jīva signs are the body and the life | BPHS 46.6, 46.94–111 |
| Aṣṭottarī | "to be considered especially for the results" of a birth by day in the dark half or by night in the bright half; also when Rāhu is in an angle or trine from the lord of the Lagna | BPHS 46.17, 46.23 |
| Ṣoḍaśottarī | "for knowing the results" when the Lagna is in the hora of the Moon in the dark half or of the Sun in the bright half | BPHS 46.24 |
| The other conditional nakṣatra daśās (Dvādaśottarī to Ṣaṭtriṃśatsamā) | each for a birth that meets a condition of its own; the verses give the condition and the count, not a purpose | BPHS 46.27–43 |
| Śūla | the time of death ("*niryāṇasya vicārārtham*"): one's own, and of the father, mother, brothers and sister or child | BPHS 46.181; Jaimini 2.3.18–25 |
| Sthira with the Brahma and the Maheśvara | the length of life: from the sign of Brahma to the sign of Maheśvara | Jaimini 2.1.55 |
| Paiṇḍī, Aṃśī, Naisargikā | the length of life; the Phaladīpikā names them with Śrīpati's daśā, the Aṣṭakavarga daśā, the Kālachakra and the Viṃśottarī as the daśās to judge the length of life by | BPHS 46.201; Phaladīpikā 13.25 |
| Aṣṭakavarga daśā | the results of the daśās of the Paiṇḍī kind are to be judged by the Aṣṭakavarga | BPHS 46.203 |
| Trikoṇa | the results are told from the "door" and "outside" signs (*dvāra*, *bāhya*) | Jaimini 2.4.25 |
| Kendrādi (the Mūla of the app) | the strength of the lords and the results as in the Chara | Jaimini 2.4.14 |
| Yogārdha, Dṛg, Maṇḍūka | no purpose is given in the passages read | Jaimini 2.4.19–23, BPHS 46.174–187 |

## Why the Viṃśottarī

Three kinds of reason, the first two from the texts and the third the writer's own inference.

1. **The texts that teach astrology name it.** BPHS 46.3 and 46.14 call it the principal daśā. The Laghu Pārāśarī (1.3, "*daśā viṃśottarī cātra grāhyā nāṣṭottarī matā*") takes the Viṃśottarī alone for
   the results and says the Aṣṭottarī is not accepted. The Phaladīpikā (22.30) says the daśā that rests on the nakṣatras "is always strong". The editor of the Uttara Kālāmṛta writes that the author put the
   Viṃśottarī first and so ended the belief that the scholars of South India favour the Aṣṭottarī, and that "it is proved by experience that the Viṃśottarī is the more able to decide the order of events".
   That last is the editor's statement, with no data behind it.
2. **It applies to every birth.** The conditional daśās (BPHS 46.17–43) are for a birth that meets a condition; the Viṃśottarī asks for none, and needs only the longitude of the Moon.
3. **The results are written for it.** *Inference of the writer, not of a text:* the daśā-result chapters of the books read are for the Viṃśottarī (Phaladīpikā 19, Jātakapārijāta 18, the Laghu Pārāśarī);
   for the Kālachakra the texts give the wheel and the omens of the jumps, for the Jaimini daśās the rule of the order and the years. A reader who wants a result for a period finds it for the Viṃśottarī.
   That the programs of today open with it is also likely, but is not shown here.

The reason the text itself gives (a full life of 120 years) is a reason for its length, not a proof that it times events better.

Mānasāgarī gives the Viṃśottarī, the Aṣṭottarī, the Sandhyā and the Yoginī daśā each with results of its own: the classical authors did not all write as if the Viṃśottarī were the only one.
